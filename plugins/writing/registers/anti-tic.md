# Anti-tic rules: intercepting the tells of machine-written prose

**Scope: every register.** The genre changes what counts as good
prose; it does not change what reads as machine-written. Keep these
rules in one file that each register names, rather than copying them
into several, where they would drift apart.

## The countable four

Advisory, not a veto: a flag is a prompt to look, not a defect. A
colon before a real enumeration is fine, and a count can't tell the
difference.

| Rule | Ceiling | Why it is a tell |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Announcement colons per 1,000 words | ≤ 1.6 | The colon used to announce rather than to list or define. The most discriminating measure found so far |
| "Not X but Y" per 1,000 words | ≤ 3.0 | Antithesis as a recurring move. Once, as a real contrast, is ordinary prose |
| Colon-led lists of three or more | 0 | A colon followed by three or more comma-separated items closing on *and* or *or* |
| Consecutive short sentences | 0 | Two or more sentences of five words or fewer in a row reads as staccato performance. A single short sentence is a legitimate beat |

**Evidence for the colon ceiling.** In one author's measurement across
ten documents, the author's own letters read 0.00–0.91 announcement
colons per 1,000 words, and model drafts read 1.86–8.45, the worst an
email. The 1.6 ceiling, inherited from the same author's scholarly
corpus, fell on the boundary.

## The manual rules

No count sees these, which is why a clean count is not a pass.

- **Figurative language and metaphor.** Literalise.
- **Ornate parallelism**, and balanced abstract closers. A paragraph
  that ends on a tidy aphorism usually ended one sentence earlier.
- **Performed candour** and manufactured concessions.
- **Sentences about the document** rather than the world. A leading
  roadmap is fine; backward and sideways narration is not.
- **Definite-article grandiosity**: "*the* institution that…", "*the*
  framework for…", where "a" is true and "the" is a claim.
- **The rule of three everywhere.** A tricolon is not forbidden; the
  tricolon as the default shape of every list is the tell.
- **Smoothly stated unanchored specifics.** The confabulation rule and
  the tic rule meet here: fluent precision without a source is both.
- **Uniform paragraph length and sentence rhythm.** Human prose is
  lumpier.

## Per-register notes

- **Email:** contractions and a typed double hyphen (`--`) for a dash
  are correct register, not tics. The announcement colon is the live
  risk in email.
- **Academic:** em-dashes are minimised (see the academic register's
  ceiling); hedges are load-bearing and stay.
- **Applications and CVs** often invert academic habits (no hedging, no
  first-person plural). A register for them should say so, and these
  rules still apply on top.
